Why St. Petersburg needs more bike lanes?  

Walking through St. Petersburg, it’s impossible not to notice the endless lines of cars, the honking, and the smell of exhaust. Our beautiful city is drowning in traffic, and the air is getting dirtier every year. One big reason? There’s almost nowhere safe to ride a bike. Narrow lanes, aggressive drivers, and a lack of proper paths make cycling feel like a dangerous sport rather than a normal way to get around. If we want cleaner air, less noise, and happier people, we need to change this.  

Imagine a St. Petersburg where you can hop on a bike and ride safely to work, to a café, or along the Neva River without fearing for your life. We need a real network of bike lanes—wide, separated from cars, and well-lit at night. These paths should connect neighborhoods, parks, and metro stations so that cycling becomes an easy choice, not a risky adventure.  

We can make it happen very easily. At first, listen to people, ask cyclists and pedestrians where bike lanes are most needed. The worst traffic spots are obvious — start there. Start now, build a few high-quality pilot lanes in the city center. Show people how good it could be.  Then use the success of the first lanes to push for more—gradually covering the whole city.  And make cycling cool: ad campaigns, bike-sharing programs, and city-sponsored cycling events can change minds.  

Yes, there are a lot of challanges such as scepsis, for example: "There’s no space!", some streets are cramped, but if we prioritize people over parked cars, we’ll find room. "But what about winter!", yeah, snow is a problem, but cities like Oslo and Helsinki keep bike lanes clear. If they can do it, so can we.  

So, more bikes mean fewer traffic jams, cleaner air, and a city that’s alive in a new way. Kids could ride safely to school. Workers could avoid packed buses. Tourists would see St. Petersburg as not just a historic gem, but a modern, forward-thinking city. It’s time to pedal toward that future.
